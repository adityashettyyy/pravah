26,368 legs, 12 Sep to 06 Oct 2018

1. trip chains (9,986 consecutive leg pairs)
   next leg delayed after a delayed leg: 36.2%, after an on-time leg: 17.6%
   after removing each hub's own delay rate: -0.003 vs +0.000
   -> the raw gap is explained by which hub the next leg leaves from, not by a cascade

2. hub to hub, 24h buckets (96 hubs with 4+ legs/bucket, 198 edges)
   corr(upstream delay at t, downstream delay at t+1): -0.022  (n=2,079)
   same with a random hub as 'upstream':                -0.008  (n=753)
   a hub's own delay at t vs t+1:                       -0.072  (n=600)
   -> upstream hubs predict no better than random hubs

3. congestion: within-hub corr(legs leaving in the same 6h, delayed) = -0.035  (23 hubs, 7,617 legs)
   -> no load-delay relationship

4. hub delay rate, first vs second half of the period (180 hubs): corr 0.86
   corr(hub delay rate, mean rate of its neighbours) = 0.39 (801 hubs)
   corr(hub delay rate, mean rate of its state)     = 0.43
   -> delay-proneness is a stable, regional hub property; this is what a graph model can learn
